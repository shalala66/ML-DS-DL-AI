When an angle is measured in radians, for very small values, sin(x) and tan(x) become 
approximately equal to the value of the angle itself. 
In other words, for small angles, the values ​​of sin and tan are nearly equal to the 
angle's value expressed in radians. 
This follows from Taylor's formula: as x becomes very small, the terms x³, x⁵, x⁷... become even smaller.

Note:
Why x = 0?
Because at zero, the functions and their derivatives yield simpler values (specifically 0, 1, and -1) 
which simplifies the formula.
"Around x = 0" means we are creating a mathematical replica-valid with high precision as x approaches 
0, that is composed of simple powers of x.
There is a very important detail here:

1) Maclaurin series: specifically at (or around) x = 0
The Maclaurin series is the name given to the Taylor series (it's a special case of it) when 
the center is chosen as the point zero (a = 0); in other words, it is a Taylor series centered at 0.

2) Taylor series: at x = a (where a can be any number, such as 0, 1, 2, etc.)
A Taylor series can be centered at x = a; for instance, if a = 1, we are dealing with a Taylor series 
centered at x = 1, and the term (x - 1) appears throughout the formula.
